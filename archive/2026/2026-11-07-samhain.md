# Samhain

**Saturday Nov 7 · 2:52 am PT**

*Astronomical midpoint (Sun at 225°) · Traditionally Oct 31*

## Lore

In the Gaelic world, Samhain marked the end of the pastoral year. Herds were brought down from summer pasture, the harvest was done, and the dark half of the year began. Medieval Irish texts place it among the great turning points of the calendar, a time when the boundary between this world and the Otherworld was thought to grow thin.

The Gaelic day began at sunset, and the year began in the dark, so Samhain was less an ending than a threshold. Later folk custom gathered around it: hearth fires kindled anew, places set for the dead, and the old habit of staying close to home once the nights drew in. This year the astronomical midpoint falls on the 7th, a week after the traditional date, so you have some quiet room to arrive at it slowly.

## Why it matters

Symbolically, Samhain asks you to stop reaching and start keeping. The harvest is in, the light is low, and what remains is to honour what has passed and take stock of what will carry you through winter. It gives you permission to turn inward without apology.

## Reflection

Who or what has passed out of your life this year that you'd like to remember by name, and what did it leave with you?

## Small ritual

Sit in the evening with a single candle and a small piece of paper. Write the names of people, places or seasons of your life you want to remember. Light the candle, read each one quietly, and say thank you after each. Let the candle burn for a few minutes in silence. When you're ready, blow it out and keep the paper somewhere you'll see it through the winter, or tuck it into a book.

## Outside

Walk down to the Puntledge River or Nymph Falls on a grey afternoon this week. The chum are running and the eagles are gathered to feed, so you can watch the salmon's whole cycle of life and death play out in plain view. Stand for a few minutes at the water's edge and just watch.
