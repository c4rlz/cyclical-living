# Full Moon in Taurus

**Sunday Oct 25 · 9:12 pm PT**

*Tropical: Taurus · Sidereal: Aries, Ashwini · Purnima*

## Lore

In the Vedic tradition, Purnima is the full moon day, when the moon is whole and the mind is said to be at its clearest and most open. This one falls in Ashwini, the first nakshatra, ruled by the Ashvins, the divine twin horsemen who are healers and bringers of dawn. Symbolically, it is a moon of fresh starts arriving at the very moment of fullness, as if the light itself were a small sunrise.

Many families mark Purnima with a simple fast, a lamp, or a quiet offering of thanks. The old idea is modest: when the moon is full, you pause and give thanks for what has ripened before you turn toward what comes next.

## Why it matters

Late October is a season of letting go, and this moon asks you to do it with gratitude rather than effort. Ashwini's healing, beginning energy suggests that release and renewal are close companions. You can notice what has ripened this year, thank it, and let the rest fall like leaves.

## Reflection

What has quietly ripened in you this year, and what are you ready to set down now that it has done its work?

## Small ritual

Light a single candle in the evening and sit with a cup of tea. On a small piece of paper, write one thing you are grateful for on the left, and one thing you are ready to release on the right. Hold the paper for a few breaths in the candlelight. Then tear it down the middle. Keep the gratitude half somewhere you will see it, and drop the other half in a bowl of water to soften and dissolve. Pour the water onto soil in the morning.

## Outside

Visit a salmon stream such as Goldstream while the chum are still running, and stand quietly at the water's edge for ten minutes. Watch the fish, the fallen yellow maple leaves, and the way the river carries everything onward.
