# Full Moon in Taurus

**Sunday Oct 25 · 9:12 pm PT**

*Tropical: Taurus · Sidereal: Aries, Ashwini · Purnima*

## Lore

In Vedic tradition, this Purnima falls in Ashwini, the first of the twenty-seven nakshatras, named for the Ashwini Kumaras, the divine twin horsemen who are physicians to the gods. They arrive at dawn, swift and bearing remedies, and the star group is associated symbolically with healing, new starts and the first quickening after a long stillness. It is a curious thing for a full moon to sit at the very beginning of the wheel. The light is at its fullest, and the oldest story attached to it is about starting again.

## Why it matters

Late October is a season of letting go: leaves down, salmon spent, the year turning inward. A full moon in the nakshatra of beginnings suggests that release and renewal are one gesture, and that what you put down tonight makes room for what comes next. It is a good night to be gently grateful for what has already been carried through.

## Reflection

What in you is tired and ready to be tended, and what small, quick step toward mending it could you take this week?

## Small ritual

Fill a bowl with water and set it on a windowsill, or outside if the sky allows, where the moon can reach it. Sit beside it with a cup of tea. Name quietly one thing you are ready to release and one thing you are grateful to have been given this year. When you finish, touch the water with your fingertips, then pour it onto the soil of a plant or the ground outside as a small thank-you.

## Outside

Walk down to the Puntledge River near Nymph Falls in daylight on Sunday and watch for the chum salmon in the shallows, at the end of their long journey while the bigleaf maples drop yellow leaves around you. Stand a few minutes and simply watch, letting the river show you what completion looks like.

## Go deeper

Look into the Ashwini Kumaras in the Rigveda, where the twin healers appear in hymns as bringers of dawn and cures. Reading a short translation of one is enough.
